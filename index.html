02 / Product Thinking

From User Insight to Product Decision

A short walkthrough of how I move from research to a product call. Follow the four stages, or jump to the one you care about.

Hypothetical scenario A student productivity app is getting plenty of signups, but most users stop opening it within two weeks. Growth looks healthy; retention doesn't. Where would I start?

I started with five user interviews. Three insights kept recurring. Hover or tap each to see what the user said and what I noticed.

  • ?

    Confusing features

    “I signed up, saw a bunch of buttons, and wasn't sure what to tap first.”

    What I noticed: the app showed its full feature set on day one, with no sense of a starting point.

  • !

    Forgotten reminders

    “It did remind me once, but at a time I couldn't do anything about it, so I ignored the rest.”

    What I noticed: reminders were generic and badly timed, so users learned to tune them out.

  • #

    Task organization

    “Everything piles into one long list. By Wednesday I can't find the thing that's actually due.”

    What I noticed: no grouping or priority, so the list stopped reflecting what mattered now.

Next I translated what people said into the need underneath it, and the product opportunity that follows.

Confusing features on day one → “Show me where to start.” → Guided, focused onboarding
Reminders ignored → “Nudge me when it's useful.” → Reminders tuned to context & timing
One overwhelming list → “Help me see what matters now.” → Structure & prioritization for tasks

The through-line: signups aren't the problem. Users leave because the app never helps them feel oriented, on time, or in control, the three ingredients of a habit worth keeping.

Three opportunities, limited time. I weighed each on impact, feasibility, and tradeoffs before making a call.

Simpler onboarding

Impact Feasibility

Directly targets the first-week drop-off and is quick to build. Tradeoff: helps new users more than the ones already slipping away.

My pick to ship first

Personalized reminders

Impact Feasibility

Hits retention most directly: a well-timed nudge pulls lapsing users back. Tradeoff: needs usage data and careful timing, so it's more work, and done badly it becomes spam.

Better task organization

Impact Feasibility

Real long-term value for power users. Tradeoff: matters most after people already return regularly, so it's a stronger second step than first.

The decision: ship personalized reminders first. It maps to the strongest retention lever even though it's the hardest build, and I'd pair it with the low-cost onboarding fix to help the next wave of signups land better.

A decision isn't done until I know how I'd check it. Here's what I'd measure, and what I'd still need to learn.

D14 Two-week retention, the headline number this whole effort is aimed at
Open rate Reminder taps vs. dismissals, to tell a helpful nudge from spam
Time to first task How fast a new user completes one real action, to check onboarding

What I'd still want to learn

  • An A/B test of reminder timing and copy before rolling it out to everyone.
  • Follow-up interviews with lapsed users to confirm timing, not content, is the real issue.
  • A guardrail metric for notification opt-outs, so a retention win doesn't quietly cost trust.
