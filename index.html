ER FastTrack Emergency-room triage support · solo build

Emergency departments lose time to repeated information-gathering, and patients misjudge how urgent they are, roughly 65% overestimate and about 8% underestimate (the more dangerous case). The tool collects structured info before a clinician sees the patient and surfaces details that may need a faster look.

Patients report → software organizes → clinicians decide. An adaptive intake asks one question per screen; a non-diagnostic flagging engine surfaces what may need review and explains why in plain language; a nurse dashboard presents it clearly.

The defining boundary: it never diagnoses or ranks patients on its own. Every priority and clinical call stays with qualified staff, the constraint that shaped every later decision.

Gather Visual reference discovery · with Julia Hou

Finding the right reference image is mostly keyword guesswork: people scroll past near-misses and jump between Pinterest, Google, and other sites. Gather explores how AI-driven personalization and smart filtering could make that more intuitive.

We started from a user profile built from preliminary research. Maya is a creative university student who makes recreational art and runs a small business, so she needs inspiration and references she can trust and credit properly. Her frustrations shaped Gather directly: tailored results, clear sourcing, and an easier way to keep track of references.

  • Finding the right reference. Google works when users already know what they want; Pinterest for open inspiration, but recommendations often miss the mark.
  • Browsing vs. searching with a goal. People explore without a starting point, then switch to targeted search once they have an idea.
  • Trust, attribution & copyright. Tracking down proper attribution is time-consuming; users worry about stolen or AI-generated work.
  • Managing & revisiting references. References are spread across platforms, making them hard to save and come back to.
