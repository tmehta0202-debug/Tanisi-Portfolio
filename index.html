02 / Product Thinking

From User Insight to Product Decision

A short walkthrough of how I move from research to a product call. Follow the four stages, or jump to the one you care about.

How I work The same four moves run through all of my projects: understand the problem, design a response, build it, and look back to improve. Follow the stages to see how each one shows up in my actual work.

Before designing anything, I try to pin down the real problem and who it's for. Two projects started exactly here.

  • ER FastTrack Emergency-room triage support

    I read how emergency departments actually sort patients before designing anything, and built the whole thing around one rule: patients report, the software organizes, and clinical staff decide. The tool never diagnoses or ranks patients on its own.

    Shows: framing a problem with real domain constraints and a clear safety boundary. Read the case study →

  • Gather Visual reference discovery

    With my partner Julia Hou, I grounded Gather in preliminary research first, building a user profile (Maya, a creative student) and synthesizing findings into four insight clusters before touching any screens.

    Shows: starting from users and research rather than a solution. Read the case study →

With the problem framed, I move into research-driven UI/UX and prototyping, from mid-fidelity flows to polished screens.

  • Gather Research → mid-fi → hi-fi

    I carried Gather from a mid-fidelity flow through to finished high-fidelity designs in Figma, where each insight cluster mapped to a real screen, with attribution built into every result rather than bolted on.

    Shows: turning research into interaction and interface decisions. See the designs →

  • Bits of Good Bootcamp Figma design track

    Through Georgia Tech's Bits of Good, I rebuild real product screens in Figma, Apple, Duolingo, Etsy (desktop and mobile), Pinterest, and Letterboxd, using Auto Layout and studying how strong interfaces hold together.

    Shows: deliberate practice in UI craft, hierarchy, and prototyping. See the redesigns →

Design only matters once it's real. These three show collaboration, implementation, and hands-on technical work.

  • Ignition Portal Hackathon team project

    At the GT IGNITE Hackathon, our three-person team took a survey finding (44 responses; "too many platforms" was the top struggle) to a working onboarding portal. I owned research and helped drive the UI and user flows, using Claude to prototype quickly.

    Shows: teamwork and AI-assisted prototyping under time pressure. Read more →

  • Unraveling Game UI · GT VGDev

    As one UI designer inside VGDev's 100+ member team, I design start screens, menus, a paranoia overlay, and a music toggle in Figma, keeping styling dark and tense while structure stays readable, and revising to match a shared direction.

    Shows: contributing design inside a large collaborative team. Read more →

  • The Flower 2D platformer · GameMaker

    A solo game I'm building in GameMaker (GML) with hand-drawn pixel art, movement and animation, double jump, health, collectibles, hazards, and slope physics.

    Shows: end-to-end technical implementation on my own. Read more →

Looking back across the work, here's how I think about feedback, tradeoffs, and what I'd do next. The forward-looking items are clearly marked as ideas, not completed work.

Feedback & iteration On Unraveling I revise my UI based on feedback from a 100+ person team; on Gather, research clusters shaped the design before I committed to screens.
Prioritization & tradeoffs ER FastTrack is the clearest tradeoff: I deliberately kept the software non-diagnostic, accepting less "smart" behavior in exchange for safety and clinician trust.
Hypothetical next steps Ideas, not done yet: usability testing on the Gather flows, finishing and play-testing The Flower, and expanding ER FastTrack's accessibility checks with real users.

The throughline: across research, design, and build, I try to start from a real need, make a deliberate tradeoff, and stay open to revising, which is exactly the product thinking I want to keep sharpening.
