Overview

Learning interface design by rebuilding real products

I learn fastest by rebuilding things that already work, so most of my exercises take a product I know, Duolingo, Etsy, Pinterest, Letterboxd, Apple, and rework a real screen from it. Studying why the original makes the choices it does, then improving it, has taught me more about hierarchy, spacing, and components than designing from a blank canvas would.

My process is consistent across them: understand who the screen is for, rebuild it in Figma with Auto Layout so components stay responsive, and push on structure and visual hierarchy until the important things are the easiest to find.

Process

How I approach each redesign

  1. Study the original

    Look at how the real product handles layout, hierarchy, and components, and find where it gets in the user's way.

  2. Ground it in the user

    Where it helps, start from a user persona (as I did for Duolingo) so the changes serve a real need, not just taste.

  3. Rebuild with Auto Layout

    Reconstruct the screen in Figma with Auto Layout so spacing and components stay consistent and responsive as I iterate.

  4. Push on hierarchy

    Reorder and restyle until the most important content is the easiest to find, then adapt for other sizes where relevant (like the Etsy mobile checkout).

What I'm getting from it. A growing set of interface redesigns, Apple, Duolingo, Etsy (desktop and mobile), Pinterest, and Letterboxd, that each show cleaner layouts and clearer hierarchy, plus a much stronger habit of structuring a design in Figma with reusable, responsive components. It's also where Gather, my AI-powered visual discovery concept, began.
